## Direct Answer

Locksmith tools are not "a pile of lock picks" — they form an **organized system, grouped by task type**. The industry commonly divides professional locksmith equipment into **6 categories**:

1. **Essential Hand Tools** — screwdrivers, pliers, tweezers, measuring scales
2. **Lockpicking Tools** — picks, rakes, tension wrenches, broken-key extractors
3. **Key Cutting & Duplication** — code-cutting machines, duplicators, key blanks
4. **Electronic & Specialized** — automotive key programmers, Lishi tools, pick guns
5. **Safety & Misc.** — protective gear, depth gauges, borescopes
6. **Business Software** — scheduling, pricing, inventory

The core logic of this classification is **task-driven, not tool-shape-driven**. Each locksmith's toolkit is filled according to their service scope (residential / commercial / automotive / safe).

## The Six Categories in Detail

### 1. Essential Hand Tools

The foundation of all locksmith work: assorted screwdrivers, pliers, **formed-end tweezers (for picking up small pins)**, measuring scales, hammers, and so on. These involve no "lock opening" but support disassembly, repair, and installation.

### 2. Lockpicking Tools

The category the public knows best — and misunderstands most. Breakdown:

| Sub-class | Representative tools | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pick | hook / half-diamond / ball | Single-pin manipulation |
| Rake | city rake / Bogota / snake | Fast "sweep" of low-security locks |
| Tension wrench | L-shaped / Z-shaped / TOK / BOK | Apply rotational torque (the core tool) |
| Broken key extractor | — | Remove a key broken off in the lock |
| Plug spinner | — | Rotate the plug back after opening |

> ⚠️ Compliance note: this category is **open to licensed locksmiths / credentialed personnel in most jurisdictions, but carries different legal restrictions for general possession**. See our Legal Risk Matrix (Phase 1, document 01). This content is classification education only, not usage instruction.

### 3. Key Cutting & Duplication

Tools that turn "a key" into "the correct key." The core dichotomy:

- **Origination**: cut an original key from a numeric **bitting code**, with **no physical key** present. Three types: manual (hand-set depth), automatic (electronic), punch (stamped).
- **Duplication**: trace an existing key to copy it.

This sub-class gets its own deep-dive (see Related below).

### 4. Electronic & Specialized

Entering modern automotive and electronic locks:

- **Key programmer**: writes transponder chips
- **Lishi tool**: 2-in-1 pick + decoder, keyway-matched
- **Pick gun**: battery-driven, knocks pins to the shear line
- **Key decoder**: reads pin depths

### 5. Safety & Misc.

Safety glasses, protective gloves, lock/safe **borescopes** (reading wafers, illumination), **key gauges** (depth measurement), and so on.

### 6. Business Software

Scheduling, pricing/invoicing, inventory management, GPS tracking — the tools that keep the locksmith *business itself* running.

## What a Beginner Needs (Minimum Viable Toolkit)

Following "task-driven" logic, a beginner locksmith's **minimum viable set** (enough to start, not everything) typically includes:

1. **A pick set** (at least hook + half-diamond + rake)
2. **Multiple tension wrenches** (TOK + BOK, multiple thicknesses)
3. **Broken key extractor + formed-end tweezers**
4. A **key gauge** and a **plug follower**
5. A **key duplicator** or entry-level code machine (upgrade later)

The governing principle: **first acquire the tools for the tasks you can legally and safely perform, then expand as your business grows** — don't buy the full kit upfront.

## The Author's Take

**My judgment: the biggest beginner mistake in locksmith tool classification isn't "buying the wrong pick" — it's "treating the tool as the goal and forgetting the task."** Two things worth emphasizing:

1. **The tension wrench matters more than the pick** — there's an industry saying: "You might open some locks with a tension wrench alone, but you'll never open one with a pick alone." Because torque control is the core feedback source in picking; the pick is just the probe.
2. **"Origination vs duplication" is the real dividing line in the key business** — it determines which machine class you need and your service ceiling (origination handles "key lost but code on record," duplication only handles "key in hand").

(This is professional judgment based on locksmith industry tool-classification consensus, not empirical testing.)

## FAQ

**How many categories of locksmith tools are there?**
Commonly 6: essential hand tools, lockpicking tools, key cutting & duplication, electronic & specialized, safety & misc., and business software.

**What tool do beginners most often overlook?**
The tension wrench — people assume the pick is the core, but torque control is the key to picking feedback.

**What's the difference between originating and duplicating a key?**
Origination cuts a key from a numeric code with no physical key; duplication traces an existing key. The two need different machine types.
