## Quick Answer

A safe's **burglary rating** measures the **body's** resistance to forced entry (prying, drilling, cutting, torching) against a defined tool set and time budget. Two parallel systems exist and are **not directly comparable**:

- **North America** — **UL 1037** (Residential Security Container, RSC) for gun/home safes; **UL 687** (TL-15, TL-30, TRTL, TXTL) for commercial safes.
- **Europe** — **EN 14450** (S1/S2 secure cabinets) for light duty; **EN 1143-1** (Eurograde 0–XIII) for burglary-resistant safes and strongrooms.

The lock is rated separately ([UL 768 / UL 2058 / EN 1300](safe-lock-types-explained.md)) — and in the European system the required lock class is *dictated* by the body grade.

> **Definition:** A **burglary rating** is a third-party-verified classification of a container's resistance to unauthorized physical entry. It is earned by an independent laboratory attack test using a prescribed tool set within a defined time budget (UL "net working time" in minutes, or European "resistance units" RU/SU). It is a **comparative benchmark**, not a guarantee that the safe will survive a specific number of minutes in a real burglary.

---

## The Biggest Misconception: "Net Working Time" ≠ Real Break-In Time

This is the most misread number in the entire category:

- UL's "15 minutes" in **TL-15** is **net working time** — the accumulated time during which the *tool is actually in contact with the safe*, excluding every pause: repositioning, tool changes, thinking, breaks. UL testing also involves **skilled technicians** who know the container's construction `[F]`.
- A TL-15 safe can take **well over an hour of wall-clock time** for a real attacker to open — and a determined professional with unlimited time and tools can eventually open *any* container.

**What the rating actually buys you** is a raised *cost* of entry — time × skill × tooling — not a magic shield. Higher grade = higher cost of entry = the attacker is more likely to be caught, interrupted, or deterred. No rating means "unopenable."

## North America: UL 1037 (RSC) and UL 687 (TL/TRTL/TXTL)

### UL 1037 — Residential Security Container (RSC)

RSC is the residential/gun-safe tier, tested under **UL 1037, Standard for Antitheft Alarms and Devices** (not UL 687 — a common mislabel). Key facts `[F]`:

- **5 minutes** minimum resistance to hand-tool attack (hammers ≤3 lb head, chisels, pry bars, wrenches, punches, screwdrivers; no tool >18 in long).
- Attack levels scale by **attacker count and tool aggressiveness**: Level 1 = one technician, basic tools; Level 2 = two technicians, adds picks, high-speed carbide drills, pressure devices, attempting a **6-square-inch opening** in the door/front face; Level 3 = more attackers/tools for the same window.
- A **drop test** applies to units ≤750 lb that are *not* designed to be anchored.
- Construction: door at least **3/16 in** steel, body at least **12 gauge**, with a **Group 2 or Type 1** lock (see [lock grades](safe-lock-types-explained.md)).

### UL 687 — Tool-Resistant (TL) and Torch-and-Tool-Resistant (TRTL/TXTL)

Commercial safes are graded under **UL 687**. Construction floor for TL ratings `[F]`: minimum **1 in** steel body (or equivalent) with welded construction, ≥**750 lb** or anchorable, and a **Group 1/1R/2M combination lock or Type 1 electronic lock**.

| Class | Net working time | Attack surface | Tool set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TL-15** | 15 min | Door + front face | Hand tools, picks, high-speed carbide drills (≤1/2 in), cutting wheels, saws, impact tools, pressure devices |
| **TL-30** | 30 min | Door + front face | Same as TL-15 |
| **TL-15X6 / TL-30X6** | 15 / 30 min | Door + **all six sides** | Same as TL (the "X6" = six-sided) |
| **TRTL-15X6 / TRTL-30X6** | 15 / 30 min | Six sides | TL tools **+ cutting torches** |
| **TRTL-60X6** | 60 min | Six sides | Torch + tool, extended |
| **TXTL-60X6** | 60 min | Six sides | Torch + tool **+ explosives** |

`[F]` — Sources: KL Security (UL 687 class list and tool sets), Inkas Safes, Safe & Vault Store, AMSEC. Note: these requirements explicitly do **not** cover the thermal lance (burning bar) or explosives except where the TXTL grade specifies them.

## Europe: EN 14450 (S1/S2) and EN 1143-1 (Eurograde 0–XIII)

Europe measures resistance in **Resistance Units (RU / SU)**, a calculated value combining *attacker time × tool type × tool power* — every tool is assigned a RU-per-minute value (a sledgehammer contributes far fewer RU/min than a diamond crown drill), and the container must reach a target RU total for both **partial access** (a hand opening) and **full access** (removing contents) `[F]`.

### EN 14450 — Secure Cabinets (S1/S2)

The light-duty tier. Two levels, measured in **SU** (security units):

| Level | Body construction | Resistance |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Single-walled body (~3 mm steel) + double-walled door | ~10 SU |
| **S2** | Double-walled body + double-walled door | ~20 SU |

`[F]` — Sources: iTeh (EN 14450:2017+A1:2023 — S1 ≥2.00 SU, S2 ≥5.00 SU), Hartmann Tresore, Aldridge Security. Note the SU figures in circulation vary by source; the construction distinction (single vs double wall) is the reliable signal.

### EN 1143-1 — Eurograde (0 to XIII)

The main European burglary-resistance scale for safes (Grades 0–V common) and strongrooms/vaults (up to Grade XIII). RU targets for partial/full access `[F]`:

| Grade | RU (partial / full access) | Typical positioning |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | 30 / 30 | Entry-level safe |
| **I** | 30 / 50 | Home valuables |
| **II** | 50 / 80 | Home + light commercial |
| **III** | 80 / 120 | Commercial |
| **IV** | 120 / 180 | Commercial (dual-lock required) |
| **V** | 180 / 270 | High commercial |
| **VI–XIII** | (higher, strongroom/vault range) | Banking, vault rooms |

`[F]` — Sources: Hartmann Tresore (S1/S2 and Grade 0–V RU table), Atelier Boonen (Grade V = 180/270 RU), Waldis, Chubbsafes. Grades IV+ require **two locks**; lower grades typically one. Testing is performed by **independent accredited labs** (CNPP in France, VdS in Germany, ECB•S at the European level), never the manufacturer `[F]`.

### The lock is *prescribed*, not optional

The European system couples body grade to lock class (EN 1300) — this is the cleanest guard against the "great lock, weak body" mistake:

| Safe/cabinet grade | Required lock (EN 1300) |
|---|---|
| EN 14450 S1, S2 | 1 × Class A |
| EN 1143-1 Grade 0–2 | 1 × Class A |
| Grade 3 | 1 × Class B |
| Grade 4–5 | 2 × Class B |
| Grade 6–10 | 2 × Class C |
| Grade 11–12 | 3 × Class C or 2 × Class D |
| Grade 13 | 2 × Class D |

`[F]` — Source: Eurosafe "Lock Standards EN 1300". See the full [lock-type and grade map](safe-lock-types-explained.md).

## Commercial Meaning: Cash Ratings and Insurance

Burglary ratings map to **insurer cash/valuables ratings**, which vary by country and insurer — always confirm individually. Indicative UK figures `[F]`:

| Standard | Grade | Indicative cash rating (£) | Indicative valuables rating (£) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EN 14450 | S1 | 2,000 | 20,000 |
| EN 14450 | S2 | 4,000 | 40,000 |
| EN 1143-1 | Grade 0 | 6,000 | 60,000 |
| EN 1143-1 | Grade 1 | 10,000 | 100,000 |
| EN 1143-1 | Grade 2–5 | rising (≈£17.5k–£100k+ cash) | — |

`[F]` — Sources: London & Home Counties Safes, Safelock Systems, Chubbsafes. Cash ratings are insurer conventions, **not** part of the standard, and differ across markets — treat them as planning guidance, not a spec.

## Lock Grade vs Body Grade (Do Not Conflate)

| Dimension | Measures | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Lock grade** | Non-destructive opening (manipulation, X-ray, ESD/RF) | UL 768 / UL 2058 / EN 1300 |
| **Body grade** | Forced entry (prying, drilling, cutting, torch) | UL 1037 / UL 687 / EN 14450 / EN 1143-1 |
| **Fire rating** | Heat resistance (separate, not burglary) | UL 72 / EN 1047-1 / NT Fire 017 |

A Group 1R lock on an RSC body only guarantees the *lock* resists manipulation — the body is still a 5-minute residential container. A TRTL-60X6 body with a Group 2 lock is only as manipulation-resistant as that lock. **Buy the two grades as a matched pair.**

## The Author's Take

**My position: the two most damaging buyer mistakes are (1) reading "net working time" as a stopwatch promise, and (2) buying a North American TL rating or a European Eurograde without understanding they are different systems with different tool sets — so "TL-30" and "Grade III" are not convertible.** Three points:

1. **Ratings are comparative benchmarks, not survival guarantees.** "TL-15" means "15 net minutes against a defined tool set by a skilled tester," not "15 wall-clock minutes against any burglar." Every safe yields eventually; the rating buys you raised *cost* of entry, which is what deters and buys response time.
2. **The European system is the more foolproof one for ordinary buyers**, because it *forces* the lock grade to match the body grade (Grade 0–2 → Class A, Grade 6+ → Class C, etc.). North American buyers have to make that pairing check manually — and many don't, which is exactly how "great lock, weak body" safes get sold.
3. **Residential buyers over-buy.** For a home or gun safe, RSC (UL 1037) or EN 14450 S2 is the rational ceiling. TL-30, TRTL, and Eurograde IV+ are commercial/banking tiers — you are paying for resistance to professional tool attacks and torch/explosive sets that a residential burglary does not mount.

**Conclusion:** decide the threat first (smash-and-grab vs professional tool attack), pick the body grade in the system your insurer recognizes, then let the body grade dictate the lock grade. Never memorize cross-system "equivalents" — verify the grade against the standard that issued it.

(Disclosure: this is the author's professional interpretation of the published UL 1037/687 and EN 14450/1143-1 standards plus insurer/trade documentation cited below. Cash ratings are insurer conventions that vary by market. Not first-party test data or legal/insurance advice.)

## FAQ

**Is "TL-15 = 15 minutes to break in" true?**
No. The "15" is net working time (tool-on-safe time), excluding pauses, by a skilled tester. Real-world opening typically takes much longer.

**What's the difference between UL 1037 and UL 687?**
UL 1037 covers Residential Security Containers (RSC, gun/home safes, 5-min hand-tool resistance). UL 687 covers commercial TL/TRTL/TXTL safes with longer time budgets and heavier tool sets.

**What is Eurograde (EN 1143-1)?**
The European burglary-resistance scale, Grades 0–XIII, measured in Resistance Units (RU) for partial and full access. Grades 0–V cover most safes; VI–XIII cover strongrooms/vaults.

**What does EN 14450 S1 vs S2 mean?**
Two light-duty secure-cabinet levels. S1 = single-walled body + double-walled door; S2 = double-walled body and door, roughly double the resistance.

**How do I match a lock to a safe body?**
In Europe the body grade prescribes the EN 1300 lock class (e.g., Grade 0–2 → Class A; Grade 6+ → Class C). In North America, match a Group 2/Type 1 lock to RSC, and Group 1/1R to TL-15/TL-30 bodies.
